Case study 01 · Feb 2025

A/B Testing on ChatGPT’s Desktop App

An A/B test to validate a hypothesis I created about one of ChatGPT’s problems. The result went against my assumption, and that is part of the story.

7participants in the baseline test
2response types tested
4metrics recorded

Problem

Users who like to skim may find it difficult to do so in long, time-consuming responses.

Hypothesis

If words or phrases are highlighted according to the user’s need for a type of response, skimming becomes easier for people who prefer it.

Response types

Validating expectations (checking that a response matches what you wanted) and Clarification (getting clarity on a topic).

Test

A control prototype and a variation prototype. Each participant read and understood two responses, then completed a form.

Measured

Time to read and understand, time to complete the form, ratings of how well they understood, and the marks they scored.

Participants

7 in the baseline test: 4 aged 18–24, 2 aged 25–34 and 1 above 35.

Limiting bias

Highlights were made with ChatGPT’s help so they wouldn’t give away right answers. The quiz questions were also prepared with its help, to make them slightly harder.

Result

The control performed better than the variation on both responses, so the hypothesis was not validated. Some participants found the highlight colour difficult.

Takeaway

The existing system performs very well. The problem was real, but building the feature isn’t necessary right now, and the design solution could have been better.

This case study portrays the conduction of an A/B testing, to validate a hypothesis created by me about one of the problems of ChatGPT. Also please note that in this, the final outcome was not in favour with my assumptions and there were some issues with the newly proposed solution. Although I assure you that the process is well-executed.

How did it all start?

I always wanted to prepare case studies in a different way, and this led me to the idea of finding a problem or a scope for improvement within apps or software that I use on a daily basis.

When I opted in for ChatGPT, the main obstacle for me was to find a real user problem, because this software was designed so well that it was so hard to find such a real scope for improvement. There was also the difficulty or a need to find a problem that is well aligned with the vision of the software.

Initially, I found many of them, but either it was not as important or ChatGPT had a different idea about that, when enquired to the software itself.

So months passed by, and the best part was I got to learn a lot. Within this period, I thought that it would be better to present a test conducted as a case study. And this led me to A/B testing.

Finally, about 3 months later, I found a problem!

What was the problem?

After observing many responses given by the software, I got a feeling that the responses were filled with too much white text, making it too difficult to skim and asking users to focus-read often. Initially, with that I got a solution along to highlight important words. And the problem statement was:

“Users who like to skim, may find it difficult to do so, in long and time consuming responses.”

And the solution…

The question that stood before me was “what do I need to highlight?”. And this really was a big problem. But after a lot of thinking I got a solution!

All the time during these 3 months I always thought of classifying ChatGPT on the basis of its users, or specifically, I always grouped them on the basis of their domain of search. For example, Information Technology, Medical Science and so on. But actually the common factor was the type of response they received based on their needs.

There could be many types of responses in ChatGPT, but I found and took 2 of the most common of them, and they were:

  • Validating Expectations are the type of responses in which the need of the user is to validate if the response met their expectations. These include responses like generating essays, reports and any form of content in which the users check to see if it matches their expectations.
  • Clarification are the type of responses in which the need of the user is to get clarity on a particular topic. This also includes the need to get clarity on the difference between two things like probiotic and prebiotic, where the user need is clarity on the topic, or in this case, about the difference between them.

And the hypothesis goes like…

“If we highlight words or phrases according to the user need based on a type of response, then we can facilitate skimming for users who prefer it, because majority of the users like to skim through responses rather than time-took focused reading.”

Well, what was the plan?

For the participants of both the control and the variation, a task was given to read and understand the 2 responses that I had put on both of the prototypes as previous chats. They were also told to keep a situation in mind corresponding to the 2 responses.

The following metrics were collected and recorded for both of the groups:

  • Time taken to read and understand the response.
  • Time taken to complete the form.
  • The ratings given based on how well they could understand.
  • The marks they scored.

Time defined how much time they took for both understanding and completing, ratings indicate their feedback of how much they were able to understand, and marks reflect how much they have actually understood.

If the averages of each of them is better on the control version then I could imply that my assumptions were true. This will also indicate a better task completion rate and in turn will increase user satisfaction.

About the participants

Participants were selected in a way that it almost resembles the exact user diversity as ChatGPT’s original users, comprising “4 from 18-24”, “2 from 25-34” and “1 from above 35” age groups.

Minimising biases

Highlighting of the words was done with the help of ChatGPT itself in a way that the highlighted words don’t give away right answers but provide a whole meaning of the response that could be skimmed. Also, questions for the Q&A were prepared with the help of ChatGPT to make it slightly harder to answer.

Conducting the baseline test on the control group

The test was conducted on the control group consisting of a total of 7 participants. In this, metrics were recorded, and averages were taken to gather insights on the existing system on the 2 responses. Figure 1 shows an individual participant’s test data in the baseline test.

I could clearly state that the existing system performed very well and the results were by far the best in the way they presented the contents of a response. And this really needed to be appreciated. What were the results?

The result was actually not in favour of my assumption and did not validate the predictions made. Some of the participants also mentioned the difficulty they had with the colour of the highlight. In the 2 responses, the control performed better and produced better results than the variation.

Conclusion

I really think that the design solution could have been much better, although I must admit and appreciate the existing system’s performance. So even though the problem was kind of evident, it is not really necessary to implement such a feature at the moment, considering how well the existing system conveys information.
